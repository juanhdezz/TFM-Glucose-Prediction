# TFM - Compensating for Patient Heterogeneity Bias in Glucose Prediction Models via Data Balancing

The clinical management of diabetes is fundamentally complicated by the risk of severe glycemic excursions (hypoglycemia or hyperglycemia), which cause acute patient distress, sleep disruption, and compound the risk of long-term vascular complications. While insulin therapy remains the primary intervention, the current paradigm is primarily reactive. The shift toward proactive prediction is being driven by the integration of Artificial Intelligence (AI) with Continuous Glucose Monitoring (CGM) data. Despite the remarkable potential of these predictive models, their real-world deployment is threatened by inherent biases stemming from a failure to equally represent the full spectrum of the T1D population. Specifically, models trained on uneven data distributions potentially demonstrate degraded performance and compromised safety for underrepresented subgroups defined by patient heterogeneity factors such as age, gender, or specific clinical characteristics. To address this critical flaw, this study moves beyond mere identification and focuses on corrective action by implementing sophisticated data balancing techniques, such as SMOTER, across three diverse datasets (T1DiabetesGranada, REPLACE-BG, and DiaTrend).
