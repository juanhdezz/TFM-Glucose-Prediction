Blood glucose prediction is a key tool in the management of type 1 diabetes mellitus (T1DM), enabling the anticipation of hypoglycaemic and hyperglycaemic episodes and improving patients' quality of life. Deep learning models based on LSTM (\textit{Long Short-Term Memory}) networks have demonstrated strong performance in this domain owing to their ability to capture complex temporal dependencies in continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) signals. However, the demographic heterogeneity inherent to available patient cohorts —particularly differences in age and sex— may introduce systematic biases during training, compromising the fairness and generalisation of these models towards underrepresented subgroups.

This Master's Thesis starts from the hypothesis that demographically balancing the training set improves the clinical performance of an LSTM-based glucose prediction model, especially for minority subgroups. To test this hypothesis, an experimental balancing \textit{pipeline} is designed and implemented, applied exclusively to the training folds within a patient-wise cross-validation scheme (\textit{Group K-Fold}) that enforces strict patient separation across partitions. A taxonomy of balancing techniques —organised into random, guided, and hybrid families— is evaluated across two demographic dimensions (age and sex) alongside an unbalanced control condition, on three CGM datasets with distinct structural characteristics: \textbf{T1DiabetesGranada} (643 patients, over 30 million readings), \textbf{REPLACE-BG}, and \textbf{DiaTrend}.

The statistical analysis combines Friedman tests, Nemenyi \textit{post-hoc} comparisons, Cohen's~$d$ effect sizes, and Borda count ranking aggregation, with the aim of determining whether demographic balancing produces significant and consistent effects, and under which conditions it proves beneficial from a clinical standpoint.

\bigskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} type 1 diabetes mellitus, blood glucose prediction, continuous glucose monitoring (CGM), LSTM, demographic balancing, algorithmic fairness, time series, group cross-validation.
